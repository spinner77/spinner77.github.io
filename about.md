I'm Adele. I'm a late diagnosed AuDHD woman who is leaning into living a more authentic life. I live in Buxton, in the Derbyshire Peak District.  From my crafting room/ office, I can see Beech trees and I love to see them change with the seasons.  Beechview crafts is named after them.

A while ago I took on an allotment. The soil is wonderful, but it was badly overgrown, and I'm slowly turning it into a place to grow food, medicine and materials to make things with. The plot came with two coppiced willows, a very old elder, raspberries, rhubarb, brambles and deeply rooted weeds. At home there's an apple tree I rescued as a sapling, which now gives me apples, dye and wood for crochet hooks and a Morello cherry tree, two small ponds and many native flower species.

I spin, dye, crochet, weave baskets, twist cordage and whittle (amongst many other things!). I'm learning about each plant as I go: how it likes to grow, what it's been used for in kitchens and medicine, and the folklore that has gathered around it.  I'm also learning about the seasons and cycles of nature and how my body and mind react to the natural world.  I have struggled throughout my entire life with panic disorder and as a consequence I have never fitted in or thrived in our modern society.  Finding out that I am Autistic and have ADHD has been a distinctly profound experience.  It came at a time in my life where my children were all but flown from the nest, and perimenopause was hitting hard.  Questions about my true self, the nature of life, societal norms and expectations plus my insatiable desire to learn has brought me to what you see here today.  I hope that you can find peace, interest, inspiration, kinship and compassion here.

There will also be dabbles into ethics, philosophy, neurodivergence theory, feminism.... anything that catches my attention and seems relevant.  One of my greatest skills is seeing the bigger picture, seeing the patterns that others miss.  

## Why I write Tangled Roots

Making things by hand takes a long time. A hat is ten years of apple tree, a year of fleece, a week of dye and three evenings of crochet. I log those hours, not to put a price on them, but to notice them. A lot of that work has always been done quietly, often by women, and rarely counted. I'd like to count it.
I also want to get into the deeper questions, what makes us who and what we are, why we live the way we do.  And I hope to build a safe community here for all.
