I'm Adele. I live in Buxton, in the Derbyshire Peak District, where I can see beech trees from my window. That's where the name Beechview Crafts comes from.

A while ago I took on an allotment. The soil is wonderful, but it was badly overgrown, and I'm slowly turning it into a place to grow food, medicine and materials to make things with. There are two coppiced willows, a very old elder, raspberries, a patch of winter wheat and a lot of brambles still to tame. At home there's an apple tree I rescued as a sapling, which now gives me apples, dye and wood for crochet hooks.

I spin, dye, crochet, weave baskets, twist cordage and whittle. I'm learning about each plant as I go: how it likes to grow, what it's been used for in kitchens and medicine, and the folklore that has gathered around it.

I'm autistic and ADHD. Some days I have plenty of spoons and some days I have very few, and you'll see that in what I make and how often I make it. I've stopped pretending otherwise.

## Why I write Tangled Roots

Making things by hand takes a long time. A hat is ten years of apple tree, a year of fleece, a week of dye and three evenings of crochet. I log those hours, not to put a price on them, but to notice them. A lot of that work has always been done quietly, often by women, and rarely counted. I'd like to count it.
